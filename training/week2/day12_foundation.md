## B. Reading｜10 分钟

Many learners reread the same notes several times before a test. Rereading feels useful because the words become familiar, but familiarity does not always mean that the learner can remember or use the information. A more active method is to close the notes and try to recall the main ideas without looking. This method is called active recall.

Active recall quickly shows which ideas are clear and which ones are still weak. After checking the answer, a learner can keep a short record of one mistake and write a new example. It is also helpful to wait before trying the question again. A short delay makes the brain retrieve the information instead of simply recognising it on the page.

Rereading is not completely useless. It can help learners understand a difficult explanation before testing themselves. However, only rereading may create false confidence because the material looks familiar. Combining brief reading with active recall gives learners clearer evidence of what they can actually remember. This process helps them notice patterns, correct mistakes and make more reliable progress over time.

Questions:

1. Why can rereading feel useful?
2. What should learners do during active recall?
3. What can learners record after checking an answer?
4. Why is it helpful to wait before trying a question again?
5. TRUE or FALSE: The passage says rereading is completely useless.
6. 用一句英文概括文章主旨，并使用 `active recall` 或 `reliable progress`。

完成标准：前 5 题至少答对 4 题；第 6 题必须是完整英文句子。
